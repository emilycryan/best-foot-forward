Four months. To the day. And if I'm being honest, the reason I haven't updated here is the best possible one: I've been too busy out living life to sit down and write about it.

The foot really has been coming along nicely. I'm in physical therapy once a week and it's... *fine*. Fine, but slow, and a little unchallenging. At a certain point, a few band exercises and some standing balance work probably aren't doing much for me anymore. I know I'm supposed to lay off running for another two months, and that's fine, because I can still power hike, and believe me, I am.

This past month I spent two weeks at [the best beach on the east coast](https://www.baldheadisland.com/), getting comfortable walking on soft sand and hiking through rooty forest with the dogs. It was honestly perfect for getting the foot used to real movement: lots of bending, lots of flexing, no two steps quite the same. Now I average 4 to 6 miles a day, mostly in the woods around Palisades, and it's been gloriously uneventful.

The one nagging thing is a cranky second toe. For whatever reason, that guy has been a literal pain. Since the ortho didn't put any hardware in it, it bends ever so slightly at that second joint, and that ever so slight bend causes ever so NOT slight pain. So while it doesn't move much, when it does? YOWSA. I definitely feel it.

I also found a new shoe for the foot: the [Mount to Coast H1](https://mounttocoast.com/products/h1), a hybrid trail-and-road shoe with a decent stack height but a more rigid footbed. The idea is to keep the foot from all that soft, squishy movement, and it helps, though the cranky toe still comes and goes.

Honestly, I can't really fault it. In four months this foot has healed SO MUCH. The big, hairy stuff is a complete non-issue now, so I can't say I'm surprised that one very small, very concentrated spot is holding onto a very specific little ache. That feels like a fair trade.

So we continue on, marching toward a full recovery. I go into the [Western States lottery](https://www.wser.org/) in a month, and if I miraculously get in (SPOILER: I will not), then I'm on the hook for 100 miles, six months later. And if not, then we'll see what comes along next spring. Until then, as the Eagles said, I'm just waiting in the weeds.
